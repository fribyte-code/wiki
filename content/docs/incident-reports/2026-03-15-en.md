# Incident report 2026-03-15

All of friByte went down around 10:30 on Sunday 2026-03-15 due to a temporary
power outage.

After the power outage, the hosts came back up, but routing between them was
unstable, probably because of the firewall. Because of this, Proxmox did not get
quorum, and the VMs did not start. Most of our customer-facing services, as well
as internal services such as recursive DNS and Headscale, were therefore down.

The incident was discovered at 15:01 by the customer SRiB.
I arrived on site at 15:55, together with Rolf at around 16:30.

At first we thought it was a firewall problem, and the following was the first
round of diagnostics when I arrived:

> Dole (.32) is master for .33 (CARP)
> Doffen (.34) is fallback
>
> After rebooting the firewalls, Skaftetrynet can now reach the internet (it
> could not earlier), and the firewalls' IPs (also not possible earlier)
>
> Incoming traffic still cannot be received by (among others):
> - Pengebingen
> - Skrue
>
> Skrue can `curl vg.no`, DNS and outgoing TCP work fine
>
> I can SSH to Dole from outside (the IPs are still being routed correctly by
> UiB)
>
> The firewall ruleset (`mtime` for `/etc/pf.conf`) has not been updated since
> October (Dole) and November (Doffen)

A foolish mistake made by yours truly during troubleshooting was using tests
against a VM as a sign of network stability without first checking whether the
VM was actually up.

Eventually, we discovered that the machines had low uptime (about 6 h 30 min
when we checked), that Proxmox did not have quorum, and that Pluto had failed to
start. Rebooting Pluto was enough to restore quorum. Skrue (backup server) had
to be mounted manually on all three hosts in the Proxmox cluster.

As part of the process, we also tested switching which server owned the CARP
address, and this worked completely fine in both directions.

Lessons learned:
- External monitoring would have been smart so this could have been discovered
  faster
- It is nice to have the wiki available on GitHub (that is, an external source;
  we could also switch to Codeberg) if all of friByte goes down
- It is not always DNS
- Make sure that what you are trying to ping is actually up
- Two heads think better than one
- SRiB is our most active customer
- The `pf` log is in a binary format that must be read with `tcpdump`

-Johannes S
